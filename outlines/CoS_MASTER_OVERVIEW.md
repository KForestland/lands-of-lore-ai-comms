## WHAT THIS IS

Chronicles of Souls completes the pattern Chronicles of Time established,
rather than imitating it. The same seven structural elements, now
mirrored:

| CoT element | CoS equivalent |
|---|---|
| Three era campaigns, sequential | LoL IV (Soul Era), LoL V (Kingdom Era), LoL VI (Mirror Era) |
| Hub outside normal geography/time | Draicele — but the version that exists across all three era-states simultaneously |
| A hero who belongs to no single era | The Chronowalker's daughter (connecting hero, name not yet set) |
| Butterfly effect across eras | Same system, operating at the soul level rather than the story level |
| Final location revealing the origin | "The Completion" — the Draracle's soul working, abandoned halfway, seen from both sides of the mirror at once |
| 12 endings, cumulative choices | Same architecture, not yet detailed (mirrors CoT's own undecided endings status) |
| Hidden figure who reframes everything | The Chronowalker (Katrine) |

## THE SOUL THREAD — IT NEVER LEFT

LoL IV–VI are three different answers to one question: **what is a soul
worth?**

- **The Thomgog answer (LoL IV):** souls are seeds. Death is transition.
  Immortality through rebirth is a gift.
- **The Westernlands answer (LoL V):** dark soul energy can be transformed
  rather than destroyed, if the inheritor chooses it — this is Resonance,
  now confirmed as soul magic in `GAME5_LOL5_CLASSIC_REFERENCE.md`.
- **Valdenmere's answer (LoL VI):** immortality built with love becomes
  the thing that hollows out what it was trying to protect.

Chronicles of Souls asks the question those three answers are responding
to. The answer lives in Draicele.

## THE HUB — DRAICELE ACROSS TIME

Not the Draicele of any single era — the connecting hero accesses a
version that exists outside time, where all three states overlap:

- **Soul Era (LoL IV):** Draicele is alive. The soul-forges burn. This is
  the world the Draracle built, warm and unbroken — the state the player
  should never forget once they've seen the later, buried version.
- **Kingdom Era (LoL V):** souls have gone underground — the surface
  story (succession, inheritance, dark bloodlines) is what happens when a
  world has forgotten the soul truths LoL IV established. **The Darkreach
  Vault is the connective tissue:** confirmed here as containing a
  fragment of the original Draracle soul working, separated when the
  Ancients departed, found and built into the Ashwold Compact by people
  who didn't understand what they had.
- **Mirror Era (LoL VI):** Valdenmere's own Draicele-equivalent, deeper
  than what Eina explored — below the Binding Chamber is the mirror-world
  version of the same soul experiments, conducted at the same historical
  moment as Draicele's. **Neither version was wrong** — they were two
  halves of one working, conducted simultaneously across the mirror. The
  Draracle didn't abandon Gladstone; he was waiting for the mirror-world
  half to finish before completing the Gladstone half.

**Consistency win, no reconciliation needed:** this exactly matches what
`GAME5_LOL5_CLASSIC_REFERENCE.md` already established independently — the
Darkreach Vault being "kin to Draicele" rather than a standalone MacGuffin.
The two documents arrived at the same answer from different directions.

Hub Rooms: not detailed in the source concept doc — still an open stub.

## THE CONNECTING HERO

**Updated — a son, not a daughter.** Katrine has a son, whom she mentors
personally at Draicele — continuing the pattern of the Chronomancer once
mentoring her. Unlike Katrine's own existence, though, he isn't a paradox
or a synthesis of two eras in the same tangled way — he has an ordinary
home life to go with his extraordinary one: Katrine has settled in
Gladstone with a **hunter** as his father, a loving, unremarkable home
that stands in deliberate contrast to everything about Katrine's own
existence up to this point. He grows up with two entirely different lives
running in parallel — a normal Gladstone childhood, and a mother who takes
him to Draicele to teach him things no one else in Gladstone could
possibly teach.

**Not a paradox, a synthesis** (from the source concept doc, still holds):
where Asynchronia existed outside time because her parents came from two
different eras, Katrine's son isn't a temporal contradiction — he's the
product of two full bloodlines meeting normally, plus his mother's
extraordinary nature layered on top rather than built into his own origin
the way it was built into hers.

- **Mother's line:** Chronowalker (Katrine) → Eina → Echo Sight, temporal
  attunement, a Gladstone bloodline running through Elyra and Dawn back to
  the Thomgog soul work
- **Father's line:** an ordinary Gladstone hunter — grounding rather than
  extraordinary, worth keeping deliberately simple as a contrast to every
  other bloodline in this family
- **His own nature:** soul-sight across eras — he reads the soul-state of
  any location in any era: what the soul-forges burned, what the Ashwold
  Compact consumed, what Valdenmere's binding sealed. He's never been to
  Draicele outside his mother's lessons and knows every room in it anyway,
  because every soul that passed through left a trace, and he can read
  all of them.

**Name:** still not set — worth a dedicated naming pass, same as before.

**His relationship with the era heroes** and **his full ability list**
beyond soul-sight are not detailed beyond the above — still open stubs.

## THE ROYAL FAMILY IN GLADSTONE

By the Chronicles of Souls era, Katrine has stepped out of the shadow (per
LoL6's epilogue hook — "for good or ill") and reconnected with Eina and
Aldric, who by now are established, reigning monarchs. This creates a real
family situation worth writing into the game rather than leaving as
backstory: Eina and Aldric have an adult daughter they never raised,
who is — depending how much they're told — either an old family friend
with an unusual gift, or the literal truth. And they have a grandson they
mostly didn't know existed until he was already grown enough to be
training with his mother.

**A sketch of the dynamic, not a final scene:**

- **Eina and Katrine:** the strangeness isn't grief or anger — Eina never
  lost a daughter she knew to lose. It's something closer to vertigo: a
  fully formed adult woman, older in every way that matters than the baby
  Eina named, arriving already complete. Eina's instinct as a ruler is to
  find the right formal place for this in the succession and the court;
  her instinct as a mother is much harder to name, because "mother" isn't
  quite the shape of what she is to Katrine anymore, and both of them know
  it.
- **Aldric and Katrine:** simpler, maybe — Aldric has no throne instincts
  to complicate it. He knew, from the moment the Echo Stone showed Eina his
  face, that something about his life was already written before he lived
  it. Meeting the daughter who wrote parts of it might be the one
  conversation in the whole family where nobody is performing anything.
- **The grandson, at court:** treated as a minor royal curiosity by
  Gladstone's nobility — nobody in court knows the full truth of his
  mother, so his occasional disappearances "to study with a private tutor"
  read as eccentric rather than extraordinary. A good source of light,
  human dialogue: guards who like him, a cousin who's jealous of the
  mystery, a tutor's assistant who's certain something doesn't add up and
  is wrong about exactly what.

**Not decided:** how much Eina and Aldric are actually told about
Katrine's full nature (Ancient-adjacent, outside-time origin) versus a
softened version — worth deciding deliberately, since it affects how these
scenes can be written. Flagging rather than assuming either way.

## CORRECTION — THE CHRONOWALKER'S RESOLUTION SECTION

The source concept doc's list of "what the Chronowalker has carried"
conflates two different beings under one name — worth fixing before this
goes any further, since it's a factual error against the now-confirmed
Master Story Bible, not a style choice:

| Original line | What it actually describes |
|---|---|
| "Warned Copper in LoL V... direct cause of her own birth" | The Chronowalker (Katrine) — correct as written |
| "Raised Asynchronia in Chronicles of Time and revealed her Ancient nature only at the end" | **This is the Chronomancer, not the Chronowalker.** Katrine and Asynchronia are the same person (per the Master Story Bible — Asynchronia stabilizes into becoming the Chronowalker). She didn't raise herself. The ancient Dracoid who raised/mentored her in CoT, and whose own Ancient nature gets revealed, is the Chronomancer — a separate being |
| "Watched her own parents meet in LoL VI, could never enter the room" | The Chronowalker (Katrine) — correct |
| "Seeding Echo Stones backwards through time... from her daughter's perspective" | The Chronowalker (Katrine) — correct in spirit, though "daughter" should now read "son" per the updated Connecting Hero decision below |
| "Waiting for someone to come along who could complete the Draracle's working" | Ambiguous as written — could be either being; worth clarifying which when this section gets expanded |

This concept doc predates the Master Story Bible session in this
conversation, which is presumably why the mix-up happened — it's an easy
one to make, since both figures are "ancient, watching, shaping Asynchronia
from the shadows" in slightly different ways. Worth a clean pass through
the rest of this document for the same conflation before treating it as
final.

## THE CHRONOWALKER'S ACTUAL RESOLUTION (corrected)

She doesn't appear directly until late in Chronicles of Souls — felt only
through the connecting hero's soul-sight, traces of attention in places
that shouldn't remember anything. In the final act, she appears in
Draicele's Completion Chamber — not to instruct or explain, just present
for the choice her entire existence has been building toward.

- If the connecting hero chooses **Completion or Ascension**: the
  Chronowalker allows herself to stop watching. Whatever that looks like
  for a being outside normal time — she is finally, finished.
- If the connecting hero chooses **Preservation or Refusal**: she remains,
  but changed — no longer the working's guardian because it's no longer
  incomplete. The watching becomes a choice instead of a duty, for the
  first time in her existence.

Named (Completion, Ascension, Preservation, Refusal) but not detailed

document states the design intent clearly: *"None of these choices is
wrong. All of them cost something irreplaceable."*

## 12 ENDINGS ARCHITECTURE

Stated to mirror Chronicles of Time's structure exactly — four variables,
multiple states each, collapsed into 12 distinct outcomes. Not detailed
further here, consistent with CoT's own endings status
(`CoT_MASTER_OVERVIEW.md` — undecided, Fallout-style slideshow under
consideration). Worth deciding both systems together, not separately, once
you're ready.
