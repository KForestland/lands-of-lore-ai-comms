# GAME 2 — LANDS OF LORE: GUARDIANS OF DESTINY (1997)
## Classic Reference Document — Real-Time 3D Adventure
### Source: Westwood Studios, 1997. Compiled from verified game data for remake fidelity.

---

## OVERALL STORY

On her deathbed, Scotia — witch of the Dark Army, and once the woman
Margarithe Fiston, embittered by King Richard spurning her love and by
Gladstone's failure to protect her husband — tries to pass her twisted
power to her son, Luther. The transfer goes wrong. Instead of Scotia's
magic, Luther is left with an uncontrollable curse: he randomly
shape-shifts between human, lizard, and beast forms, with no way to stop
it.

Seeking Gladstone for help, Luther is recognized and imprisoned instead,
accused of being Dark Army himself. A transformation lets him escape, and
he flees toward the Draracle's lair, pursued by Baccata — who fought
alongside the hero of the first game and is convinced Luther is exactly as
dangerous as his mother was. The Draracle directs Luther south, toward the
Huline Jungle, in search of a cure.

The real stakes are larger than Luther's curse. His transformation carries
ancient magic that has begun waking something far older: a "Mother Beast"
growing in power, spawning twisted creatures into the world, and edging
toward the resurrection of **Belial** — an Ancient god executed eons ago
for arming the Dracoids against the Huline in a war between the two races.
Both the forces trying to revive Belial and Dawn's allies trying to stop
him need the Ancient Magic bound in Luther's curse — which makes Luther, a
man just trying to be rid of it, the single most important piece on the
board.

Luther's journey runs through three **Spheres**: the Huline Jungle (First
Sphere), the Savage Jungle and Claw Mountains (Second Sphere), and finally
the City of the Ancients, raised from the sea (Third Sphere), where Luther
confronts Belial directly and chooses what to do with the curse he carries.

**Good/Evil branching:** major choices throughout — most pivotally whether
to help or slaughter the Wild Ones tribe in the Savage Jungle — track
Luther's morality and lead to up to seven different endings, ranging from
Belial overrunning the land, to a "good" ending, to Luther becoming a
tyrant god himself if he defeats Belial but has acted monstrously along the
way. The Draracle itself is encountered differently depending on the path
taken.

## THE PROTAGONIST

Unlike the first game, there is no character selection and no party.
**Luther** is the only playable character throughout, and travels alone —
Dawn and Baccata are major NPCs, not party members.

### The Three Forms
| Form | Traits |
|---|---|
| **Human** | Average stats; the only form that can equip weapons or armor |
| **Lizard** | Small — fits tunnels other forms can't; fast; casts spells more easily; cannot cross even shallow water without drowning |
| **Beast** | Tall and heavy; can wade through deep water and cross gaps others must jump; immune to poison; very strong; cannot cast spells; only mild jumps |

Early in the game, transformation between forms is involuntary and random.
Later, Luther acquires spells to change between Human and Lizard at will;
Beast form can only be left by waiting for a random shift back.

## MECHANICS

### Gameplay Style
A real-time, first-person **adventure game** rather than a dungeon-crawler
— point-and-click interaction, inventory items used on the environment,
occasional combat, and puzzles that frequently require a specific form
(Lizard for tunnels, Beast for deep water/gaps), dragging objects or
corpses, or casting a specific spell.

### Combat
Melee attack, ranged bow, items, and spells at four power levels (a fifth
level unlocks late-game via an ancient stone or the Mantle of the
Ancients). Magic points (mana) regenerate over time, like health.

### Spells — Spell Groups (corrected)
Luther's magic isn't a flat spell list — it's organized into **groups**, each
with four standard levels plus a fifth "Ancient Magic" tier that costs an
Ancient's Stone to cast:

- **Spark** — primary offensive group and Luther's starting spell. Lesser
  Spark (L1) → Greater Spark (L2) → Chain Spark (L3) → Lightning (L4) →
  **Lightning Storm** (Ancient Magic — hits all enemies in front of Luther).
  Also used non-combat, to light torches, ignite oil, and trigger switches.
- **Fireball is not a separate spell** — it's the Spark group temporarily
  transformed. A crafted **Pyra Pod** (Mandrake Root + Tar Crystals), or
  permanently the **Ring of DrakeFire**, shifts Spark into its fire-element
  form for a limited time: Lesser Fire Spark (L1) → Greater Fire Spark (L2)
  → Fire Ball (L3) → Greater Fire Ball (L4) → **Fire Storm** (Ancient
  Magic — unlike Lightning Storm, this hits only a single target).
- **Prism** — light-manipulation group: Blind (L1) → Invisibility (L2) →
  Hologram (L3) → Sunray (L4), with an Ancient Magic tier (**Trinity**).
- **Summon** — Lesser Imp (L1) → Greater Imp (L2) → Ancient Imp (L3) → Imp
  Lord (L4) → **Banish** (Ancient Magic).
- **Heal** — starting spell; its Level 2 also functions as a Damage Shield.
- **Mist of Doom** — separate offensive/utility group, used both in combat
  and to dispel certain magical obstacles.
- **Curse Control** — lets Luther shift between Human and Lizard at will;
  a Level 5 tier is used at the endgame to expel or transfer the curse.
- **Damper** — given by Dawn; a standalone effect that dampens involuntary
  morphing, not a full leveled group.

### Alchemy
A crafting system exists — combining found reagents produces new items
(e.g. Amber + Ironwood Sap = Champion Stone; Black Lichen + Gorgonite
Nodules = Stone of the Gorgon).

### Notable Items
Speaking Stones (playback historical events), Guardian Orbs, Ancient Magic
Stones, the Dreamstone, Control Tokens, the Skull Key, and named weapons
such as the Prism Sword and Thohan's Great Sword.

## THE PROTAGONIST & FORMS

Luther remains solo, matching the locked decision. The **Human / Lizard /
Beast** three-form curse system is core to the character and should be
preserved exactly — this isn't a mechanic to modernize away, it's the
identity of the entire game. Form-locked puzzle logic (Lizard-only tunnels,
Beast-only water crossings) carries over as design intent even where the
literal geometry is rebuilt.

## MECHANICS

### Spells
- Keep the group structure intact — Spark, Prism, Summon, Heal, Mist of
  Doom, Curse Control — including the Spark/Fireball relationship (Fireball
  as a temporary transformed state of Spark, not a separate spell) and
  Spark's Ancient Magic tier producing Lightning Storm. This distinction is
  worth preserving deliberately in the modernized version rather than
  flattening it into a simple spell list, since it's a genuinely elegant
  piece of the original design.
- Same QOL-addition approach as LoL1 — open for new spells, none decided
  yet

### Good/Evil Branching
- Endings aren't locked yet on the Chronicles of Time side — still an open
  design question there (a Fallout-style slideshow-with-pictures approach
  is one option under consideration, not decided). Given that, this section
  isn't ready to resolve either — how Luther's original 7-ending branching
  system relates to whatever CoT lands on for its own ending presentation
  is a question for once that broader decision is made, not before.

### Crafting & Items
- "Alchemy" becomes **Crafting** in the modernized version — same
  underlying system (combine reagents to produce new items/effects,
  including the Pyra Pod that flips Spark into Fireball), just renamed to
  fit a modern UI/UX convention rather than the original's alchemy framing.
- Named items carry over unchanged: Speaking Stones, Guardian Orbs,
  Dreamstone, the Prism-line weapons, etc.

## ART DIRECTION

This is the visual reference point for the whole Chronicles of Time
project (already established in LoL1's Part 2) — so rather than describing
a target to move toward, this section is about **preserving what already
worked**: full 3D presentation (not sprites), moving from the original's
largely static/pre-rendered adventure-game backdrops to fully realized,
freely explorable environments at higher fidelity, while keeping the same
tone and palette that made the original distinctive.

## CoT TIE-IN — THE ASYNCHRONIA THREAD

**Locked for this game:** a hidden vista in **Draracle's Cave** — a spot
the player can find that looks back out across the distance toward
Gladstone, with the White Tower visible as a small landmark on the far
horizon. It doesn't need Asynchronia present in the shot at all; the tie-in
here is geographic, quietly confirming the whole world is one continuous
place across games, not disconnected level sets. Understated on purpose —
the kind of detail only a careful player notices.

Whether a personal Asynchronia sighting (seen-and-gone, as opposed to this
vista) also belongs somewhere in LoL2 is still open — worth a dedicated
continuity pass once all three CoT games have their reference docs written,
so her appearances can be placed consistently across the trilogy rather
than one game at a time.
