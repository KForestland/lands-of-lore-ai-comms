# GAME 1 — LANDS OF LORE: THE THRONE OF CHAOS (1993)
## Classic Reference Document — 2.5D Original
### Source: Westwood Studios, 1993. Compiled from verified game data for remake fidelity.

---

## OVERALL STORY

King Richard LeGrey, 40th heir to the throne of Gladstone Keep, rules over
the lands protected for generations by the White Army. Word reaches him
that Scotia — an old witch and leader of the Dark Army — has finally found
what she's searched for across the Urbish Mines: the Nether Mask, an
artifact that lets its wearer take any shape or form.

The King sends his chosen champion out to retrieve the Ruby of Truth from
Roland, the one artifact with any hope of countering the Mask. By the time
the champion reaches Roland, he is already dying — the Ruby has been stolen
out from under him. Returning to Gladstone, the champion arrives just in
time to see Scotia infiltrate the keep and poison King Richard before
escaping. Only the intervention of the sorceress Dawn keeps the King alive.

With Gladstone in crisis, the champion seeks out the Draracle — an ancient
oracle — for the recipe of an elixir that can save the King. Gathering its
ingredients means crossing the length of the kingdom: through forests,
swamps, mines, and ruined cities, learning more about the Nether Mask at
every stop. Along the way the champion recovers the Ruby of Truth and later
a Shard of Truth, which combine into the Whole Truth — the weapon finally
capable of ending Scotia for good at Castle Cimmeria.

## MECHANICS

### Skills
Three skills, each improved purely by using them (no manual allocation):
- **Fighter** — melee combat effectiveness
- **Rogue** — bow use and lock-picking
- **Mage** — spellcasting effectiveness and speed

### Spells
Seven usable spells, each with four power levels (higher levels cost more
mana and deal more damage/effect, and are unlocked by finding scrolls or
raising Mage skill):

| Spell | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Spark** | Basic low-cost attack spell |
| **Fireball** | Fire damage, area of effect; can ignite gas pockets in the mines to open new passages |
| **Lightning** | Electrical damage |
| **Freeze** | Crowd-control/damage — effective against certain enemy types (e.g. rock creatures) |
| **Mist of Doom** | Poison-type area spell — strong against orcs |
| **Heal** | Restores health; also cures poison |
| **Hand of Fate** | Utility/attack spell |

Spells are learned via scrolls found in the world (often in chests, hidden
niches, behind puzzles) rather than freely chosen — availability is tied to
exploration, not character build.

### Tools & Items
- **Lantern** — required for dark areas (mines, caves)
- **Lock picks** and assorted keys — dungeon traversal
- **Medicine/potions** — healing, poison cure
- **Rings** — passive bonuses (e.g. health regeneration)
- **Vaelan's Cube** — dispels Scotia's magical barriers
- **Spell devices/wands** (Fireball Wand, Wand of Lightning, Wand of Death,
  Ebony Staff, etc.) — cast a fixed spell using charges instead of mana
- **Silver Crowns** — currency for shops (Victor's Arms, Nathaniel's Herbs,
  Gorkha armorer Grent, Yvel's fletcher)

### Combat & Exploration
- Real-time, first-person, grid-based movement (Dungeon Master / Eye of the
  Beholder-style engine)
- Enemies can poison or stun party members
- Resting restores HP/mana over time but can be interrupted by monsters
- Levels are dense with switches, levers, pits, and teleporters used for
  puzzles as much as combat
- A map screen shows the player's current location and points of interest

## LOCATIONS & MAPS

Same 14 locations, same connective order, same plot points as Part 1 above —
nothing added, removed, or reordered.

Map geometry gets a full 3D redesign rather than a literal recreation of the
original's tile-grid mazes. Reference **Lands of Lore III's** approach to
environment scale and architecture — open, structurally believable spaces
with real verticality — as the target feel, rather than one-tile-wide
Dungeon Master-style corridors. The original **puzzle logic** (switches,
pits, teleporters, secret walls) carries over intact; only the geometry
expressing it is modernized.

**⚠️ Reconciliation flag:** `02_PHASE1_SCOTIA_DEMO.md` currently has Timothy
joining in Northland Forest and skips Roland's Manor, Draracle's Caves, and
the Lake Dread crossing entirely before Gladstone falls — this doesn't match
the classic order in Part 1. Under "same plot points at each location," this
needs a decision: either correct the demo doc to match, or explicitly mark
it as an intentional scope-cut for a 4–6 week demo (reasonable, since fitting
all 14 locations into a demo isn't realistic). Worth settling before Phase 2
starts, since Phase 2 is billed as the full campaign.

## HERO SYSTEM

One customizable hero, not four fixed choices. At creation, the hero picks
an initial **specialization** — echoing the four originals' archetypes
(Warrior-leaning, Rogue-leaning, Mage-leaning, or Balanced) as a starting
lean rather than a locked-in character.

**Open question — flagging rather than assuming:** what becomes of Ak'Shel,
Michael, Kieran, and Conrad as characters? Two natural paths:
- **(a)** They become recruitable companions instead of alternate
  protagonists — keeps their personalities in the game, gives a narrative
  reason for the 3-person party
- **(b)** They're retired as characters; only their archetypes survive as
  specialization flavor

This matters because it interacts with the companion roster the Phase 1 doc
already has (Timothy, Baccata, Lora) — would the four originals join that
same pool, replace it, or run alongside it? Worth a deliberate call.

Party size and composition otherwise match what's already locked in
`MASTER_OVERVIEW.md`: hero + 2 companions max.

## MECHANICS

### Spells
- Keep the original charge-based casting (hold to build a charge, release to
  cast) — matches the `SpellCharge` system already named in
  `01_TECHNICAL_SPEC.md`
- Keep all 7 original spells faithfully: Spark, Fireball, Lightning, Freeze,
  Mist of Doom, Heal, Hand of Fate
- Add new QOL spells on top of those 7 — none locked in yet. Candidates
  worth considering: a **Light** spell (less reliance on physically
  carrying/refueling the Lantern), a **Detect** spell (reveals secret doors/
  traps instead of blind searching), a **Recall/Waypoint** spell (given how
  backtrack-heavy the original dungeons are). Flagging as options, not
  decisions.

### Skills
- Retool Fighter/Rogue/Mage away from the original's opaque "use it to raise
  it" grind
- Not specifying a replacement yet — "more sensible" could mean a visible
  XP/level system per skill, a proper tree with chosen unlocks, or something
  else. Worth its own dedicated pass rather than guessing here.

### Tools
- Unchanged: Lantern, lock picks, keys, medicine, rings, Vaelan's Cube,
  wands, Silver Crowns — all carry over as-is.

### CoT Tie-In — The Asynchronia Thread
- Hints placed at the White Tower — fits well, since it's already a strange,
  layered location with a hidden sub-level only reachable by falling through
  a pit from the top floor
- A young Asynchronia appears briefly in unexpected places throughout the
  game and vanishes before she can be approached — a recurring, unexplained
  figure, not a companion or quest-giver. Players won't understand what
  they're seeing on a first playthrough; it only pays off once Phase 5
  exists. This isn't a retcon — per the Master Story Bible thread already
  locked, she's genuinely alive and present during the CoT era, so glimpsing
  her at the margins of LoL1 is just showing something that was already true.
- Exact number and placement of appearances is a later pass, not decided
  here.
